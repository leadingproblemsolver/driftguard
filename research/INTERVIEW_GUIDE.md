# DriftGuard interview and usability guide

## Screening

1. What kinds of work do you review before it is approved, shipped, or sent?
2. How often does that work contain explicit requirements, acceptance criteria, or non-negotiable constraints?
3. When did a missed or weakened requirement most recently create rework or risk?

## Current workflow

1. Walk through the last review you completed from the moment the work arrived.
2. Where did you look for the governing requirements?
3. Which requirements were easy to verify and which required interpretation?
4. How did you handle missing or contradictory evidence?
5. What did you record about the reason for the final decision?

## Recent behavior and workarounds

1. Show or describe the documents, checklists, tickets, or tools used in that review.
2. Which part required the most reconstruction or memory?
3. Where do reviewers commonly disagree?
4. What happens when the evidence is incomplete but the deadline is close?
5. What is the cost of a false approval? What is the cost of an unnecessary block?

## Product test tasks

1. Define purpose, workflow, target, and observable proof for a realistic review.
2. Inspect proposed guardrails and accept, edit, or reject them.
3. Configure one blocking rule and one non-blocking rule.
4. Evaluate a supplied example containing a clear violation.
5. Evaluate an example with missing evidence.
6. Explain the verdict and correction in your own words.
7. Review the stored constraint snapshot and describe what you would need for audit or handoff.

## Outcome questions

1. Which result, if any, would change your current review decision?
2. Where did you distrust or correct the system?
3. Was the smallest correction actionable?
4. Would this add or remove steps from your actual workflow?
5. What evidence would you require before using it on consequential work?
6. Which decision should remain entirely human-owned?

## Interview constraints

- Ask about recent behavior before opinions.
- Do not ask whether the participant “wants AI.”
- Do not imply the product is compliant, authoritative, or production-proven.
- Record observed confusion separately from stated preference.
- Obtain consent before preserving quotations or recordings.
